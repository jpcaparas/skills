# Content And Entity Patterns

Use this when planning, editing, or reviewing content for Google Search and Google AI Search visibility.

## People-First Content Filter

Before optimizing, ask:

- Would a visitor feel satisfied after reading this page?
- What original information does this page provide?
- Does the author, company, or product have direct experience with the topic?
- Is the page easy to scan and understand?
- Does the media help the decision or explanation?
- Are claims supported with visible evidence, examples, data, or sources?

If the page is a generic restatement of common knowledge, technical polish will not make it strong.

## Entity Clarity

Make the real-world subject unambiguous:

- name, alternate names, brand, organization, person, product, service, location
- relationships between entities, such as product-to-brand or author-to-organization
- facts that users need to compare, choose, or act
- dates, version numbers, prices, availability, ingredients, compatibility, or service areas where relevant
- consistent entity names across title, headings, body, internal links, structured data, and images

Structured data can reinforce entity clarity, but visible content must carry the meaning first.

## Page Structure For AI Search

Use clear sections that match human tasks:

- summary of the answer or offer
- eligibility, pricing, availability, requirements, or constraints
- evidence, comparison, examples, or first-hand notes
- images/video near the text they support
- next action, related reading, product, booking, or contact path
- update date when freshness is meaningful

Do not force every section into a FAQ. FAQs are useful only when questions and answers are genuinely the best format for the user.

## Fan-Out Coverage Pattern

For a topic that triggers related questions, improve the main page with naturally adjacent coverage:

1. List the main user jobs and follow-up questions.
2. Group related questions into sections.
3. Add original detail for each section.
4. Link to deeper pages only where a deeper page has distinct purpose.
5. Remove sections that exist only to capture a keyword variant.

This supports query fan-out without creating scaled content abuse risk.

## Media Pattern

Use images and video when they make the content more useful:

- product photos from multiple angles
- screenshots of UI or workflow steps
- short demonstrations, transcripts, or captions
- diagrams for process or architecture
- charts/tables for comparisons

For implementation, ensure media is crawlable, descriptive, and close to relevant text. Add alt text that explains the image's relationship to the page, not a keyword dump.

## Internal Links

Internal links should help users and crawlers understand page relationships:

- link from hub pages to important detail pages
- link from detail pages back to relevant hubs
- use descriptive anchor text
- avoid hiding important links behind non-crawlable controls
- avoid sitewide footer spam for unrelated pages

## Generative AI-Assisted Content

Google's policies focus on helpfulness and spam behavior, not whether a tool assisted creation. If generative AI tools are used:

- verify facts with primary sources or direct experience
- add original human judgment, data, examples, or screenshots
- remove generic filler
- keep authorship and accountability clear where the site uses bylines
- reject mass generation whose primary purpose is manipulating Search visibility
