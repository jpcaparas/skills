# Time-Based Media Dissection

Use this reference for video, screen recordings, animation, audio, and transcripts. Reconstruct the whole temporal arc rather than one representative frame or a transcript-only summary.

## Establish the Timeline

Collect duration, dimensions, frame rate when useful, audio presence, chapters, captions, and transcript availability. Then inspect:

1. the opening state and how quickly the subject establishes itself
2. every meaningful scene, view, interaction, or musical section
3. transitions and the state before and after each one
4. representative frames inside longer stable passages
5. the ending state, loop point, resolution, or call to action

Sample around cuts, gestures, state changes, reveals, and audio events rather than at arbitrary equal intervals alone. For screen recordings, distinguish pointer movement from actual product feedback.

## Capture Temporal Evidence

Record:

- sequence and duration of major beats
- camera, viewport, crop, parallax, zoom, pan, and spatial continuity
- object or interface motion, easing character, anticipation, overshoot, and settling
- transitions, masks, cuts, wipes, dissolves, and persistent elements
- speech, ambience, music, silence, sound effects, and synchronization
- exact short labels or phrases essential to the experience
- interaction triggers and resulting UI states in screen recordings
- captions, visual-only cues, and nonvisual feedback

A transcript proves spoken words, not framing, visual state, speaker identity, emphasis, music, or timing unless those are encoded separately. Audio alone does not establish a visual system; require the future session to create one deliberately rather than implying it came from the source.

## Prompt Translation

For a time-based target, express a beat-by-beat arc with timing and transition intent. For a website or app target, translate temporal evidence into:

- interaction choreography
- progressive disclosure
- state transitions and feedback
- motion language
- ambient sound or silence where appropriate
- preserved content and narrative order

State what the source demonstrated and what the target adapts. Do not require a website to mimic a video literally when the user asked for an interactive interpretation; preserve identity, sequence, and emotional rhythm while making the adaptation usable.

**Complete when:** the beginning, middle, end, transitions, sound layer, and meaningful state changes are represented, and transcript evidence has not replaced audiovisual inspection.
