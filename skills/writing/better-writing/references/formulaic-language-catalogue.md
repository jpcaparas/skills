# Formulaic-language catalogue

Use this reference when a draft contains canned, model-associated, corporate, academic, promotional, or assistant-like diction. It is a general-purpose editorial catalogue, not an authorship test. It applies only to editable natural-language prose. Code and other machine-readable constructs are outside its scope; in mixed documentation, revise the explanation and preserve those constructs exactly. `Gap` and `shift` receive worked examples because they are easy to ban carelessly; they are only two members of a much broader set of phrase families.

The catalogue can be used across prose genres, but no action is genre-blind. Its force depends on the phrase's job:

| Action | Default |
|---|---|
| **Remove** | Delete the wrapper or stage direction unless the destination genuinely needs it. |
| **Rewrite** | Rebuild the claim from supported meaning. Do not leave the formula in finished prose merely because it occurs once. |
| **Review** | Inspect density and function. Keep a precise or characteristic use. |
| **Protect** | Preserve quotations, code, identifiers, official names, exact technical or legal terms, measured uses, and deliberate writer-owned language. |

`Gap`, `shift`, `key`, `critical`, `robust`, and `landscape` are not banned strings. Their vague rhetorical uses may be removed or rewritten, while exact uses remain. Ordinary connective words such as `across`, `within`, `while`, and `particularly` are density signals at most and must never be banned alone. A general-purpose skill must still be able to write about a pay gap, a night shift, critical care, robust statistics, a physical landscape, or movement across a boundary.

## The meaning-first rule

Never satisfy an avoidance rule through synonym substitution alone.

- Do not replace `gap` with `divide`, `disconnect`, `chasm`, or `mismatch` and leave the thought hidden.
- Do not replace `shift` with `transition`, `evolution`, `transformation`, or `change` and leave the before and after unnamed.
- Do not replace `landscape` with `ecosystem`, `space`, `realm`, or `environment` and keep the same empty topic container.
- Do not replace `delve into` with `dive into`, `unpack`, or `explore` when the sentence can begin with the subject.
- Do not replace `pivotal` with `critical`, `crucial`, or `key` when the consequence already shows the importance.
- Do not replace `unlock` with `unleash`, `tap into`, or `realise` when the benefit has no mechanism.
- Do not replace `robust` with `resilient`, `comprehensive`, or `enterprise-grade` when no test condition is named.

Recover the buried proposition instead. Use what the source supports:

1. actor
2. action
3. object
4. old state and new state
5. mechanism or cause
6. measure, baseline, or comparison
7. consequence for a named reader or user
8. date, condition, limit, or uncertainty

If the source supplies none of these, delete or narrow the claim. Ask for missing evidence when the claim is necessary. Never invent a mechanism to make a vague line sound concrete.

## Change, absence, and topic containers

| Action | Family | Weak when | Rewrite from | Protect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rewrite | `bridge`, `close`, `fill`, or `address the gap` | the endpoints, size, cause, or affected actor are missing | what is absent; between which states; who cannot do what; the measured difference | pay gap, air gap, band gap, gap year, physical gap, defined evidence or research gap |
| Rewrite | `marks`, `signals`, `reflects`, or `represents a shift`; `paradigm shift`; `broader shift` | the old state, new state, actor, date, or reach is missing | before and after; changed rule or behaviour; dated evidence; practical consequence | work shift, gear shift, Shift key, phase shift, distribution shift, documented realignment |
| Rewrite | figurative `landscape`, `realm`, `space`, `sphere`, `domain` | scenery stands in for the market, field, institution, or problem | the actual industry, system, organisations, rules, or event | land, airspace, mathematical domain, named technical domain |
| Review | figurative `ecosystem` | companies, tools, customers, and regulators blur into one fashionable noun | participants and their dependencies | biological systems or a genuinely named platform ecosystem |
| Rewrite | `journey`, `path`, `roadmap`, `chapter`, `embark on` | ordinary work receives an unearned motivational arc | stage, owner, date, next action, changed decision | travel, a real product roadmap, book chapter, writer-owned metaphor |
| Review | stock metaphors such as `secret sauce`, `north star`, `silver bullet`, `symphony`, or figurative `load-bearing` | the image supplies importance or coordination without mapping cleanly to the subject | criterion, constraint, dependency, sequence, or combined effect | literal objects, an analogy that genuinely explains, writer-owned imagery |
| Rewrite | `lens`, `prism`, `through the lens of` | interpretation is announced but never applied | criterion, theory, changed weighting, resulting conclusion | optics or an accurately applied analytical method |
| Rewrite | abstract `tapestry`, `fabric`, `weave`, `mosaic` | decoration replaces the relationship among influences | components, chronology, conflict, dependency | textiles, art, deliberate literary imagery |
| Review | `intersection`, `nexus`, `interplay`, `confluence` | several forces supposedly meet but no interaction is described | which factor affects which outcome; reciprocal effect; meeting point | roads, graphs, interaction terms, defined interdisciplinary work |
| Rewrite | `complexities`, `dynamics`, `nuances`, `intricacies`, `multifaceted nature` | sophistication is announced instead of shown | competing constraints, exception, mechanism, remaining uncertainty | nonlinear dynamics or a summary of complexity already demonstrated |
| Review | vague `narrative`, `conversation`, `discourse`, `debate` | no participants, position, publication, or meeting is named | speakers, positions, disputed proposition, source, chronology | narratology, recorded debate, linguistic discourse |

### `Gap`: ask these questions

- What exactly is absent, unequal, delayed, or failing?
- Which two states, groups, systems, or measurements are being compared?
- How large is the difference, in what unit, and over what period?
- Who experiences the consequence?
- Which action would change it?

Before:

> The platform bridges the gap between strategy and execution.

After, when the source supports it:

> Each approved initiative becomes assigned work with an owner and due date.

Keep:

> The pay gap narrowed from 12% to 8% between 2024 and 2026.

Keep or restate precisely:

> No published trial has compared seven- and 14-day regimens in adults over 70. That is the evidence gap this study addresses.

### `Shift`: ask these questions

- What was true before?
- What is true now?
- Who changed the rule, system, or behaviour?
- When did it change?
- Is the claim observed, measured, reported, or inferred?
- What consequence follows for this reader?

Before:

> The policy marks a significant shift towards proactive management.

After, when the source supports it:

> From July, managers will review failure signals each morning instead of waiting for customer reports.

Keep:

> Traffic shifted to the eastern bridge after the western approach closed.

Keep:

> The phase shift was 12 degrees at 50 Hz.

| Review | `innovative`, `cutting-edge`, `groundbreaking`, `state-of-the-art`, `novel` | compare with prior work or a benchmark; remove unverified novelty | supported academic or benchmark claims |
| Rewrite | generic `vibrant`, `rich`, `diverse`, `dynamic`, `breathtaking`, `renowned` | give sensory, demographic, institutional, or attributed detail | literal colour, wealth, motion, measured diversity |

| Review | promotional `boasts`, `features`, `offers`, `garnered` | use `has`, `includes`, or `received`, then give the count or function | literal boasts/offers, feature nomenclature |
| Review | vague `align`, `ensure`, `adhere`, `comply` | name the standard, clause, owner, test, and result | geometry, invariants, formal compliance |
| Review | `craft`, `curate`, `tailor` for routine production | name the writing or selection task and criteria | physical craft, museums, clothing, genuine editorial selection |

Also review clusters of prestige diction that award a verdict before supplying a test: `world-class`, `best-in-class`, `industry-leading`, `state-of-the-art`, `future-proof`, `unprecedented`, `exceptional`, `instrumental`, `poised to`, `burgeoning`, `nascent`, `quintessential`, `overarching`, `synergy`, `thought leadership`, and `commitment to excellence`. Keep a term of art or a sourced comparison. Otherwise name the benchmark, date, constraint, action, or result.

Before:

> Our robust platform empowers modern teams to collaborate seamlessly and unlock their full potential.

After, when supported:

> Reviewers comment, approve, and compare revisions in one queue. The release owner no longer reconciles three email threads.

Before:

> Nimbus ensures reliability at scale.

After, when supported:

> In staging, Nimbus processed 8,000 jobs in 14 minutes and retried each failed idempotent request twice.

If those test details do not exist, remove the assurance. Do not fabricate them.

## Openings, assistant residue, email, and endings

Remove these defaults from finished artefacts unless the named genre exception applies:

| Action | Family | Better opening or ending | Possible exception |
|---|---|---|---|
| Remove | `In today's fast-paced/rapidly changing world`; `in an era where`; `ever-evolving landscape` | start with the event, reader problem, decision, or dated trend | quotation or parody |
| Rewrite | `has emerged as`; `has become increasingly`; `in recent years`; `growing recognition` | give the date, adopter, count, or earlier and current state | sourced longitudinal reporting |
| Remove | `Imagine a world where`; panoramic `From X to Y`; `Few topics are as`; `X is more than` | use a source-backed scene, fact, tension, or thesis | deliberate rhetoric suited to the genre |
| Remove | `Certainly!`, `Absolutely`, `Great question`, `You're absolutely right` | answer directly or acknowledge the specific correction | real personal conversation |
| Remove | `Here is a comprehensive breakdown`; `Here is your polished version`; restating the request | lead with the result or an informative heading | one necessary scope orientation |
| Remove | `I hope this message finds you well`; `I wanted to reach out`; `kindly`; `at your earliest convenience` | make the request, deadline, and dependency visible | house style, cultural courtesy, writer-owned warmth |
| Remove | `I hope this helps`; `feel free to ask`; `let me know`; `happy to help`; `I can also`; `Would you like me to...`; `please do not hesitate` | end after the useful content or give the required next action | an actual support conversation |
| Review | `Thank you for reaching out`; `We understand how frustrating`; `Rest assured` | acknowledge the specific event or remedy without scripted empathy or unsupported reassurance | a sincere support reply in the organisation's established voice |
| Rewrite | blanket validation such as `That makes complete sense`; `Your instincts are right`; `You're not overreacting`; `You did nothing wrong` | acknowledge the specific concern, separate feeling from contested fact, and state what the evidence does and does not establish | genuine support where the conclusion is warranted |
| Remove | `as of my knowledge cutoff`; vague `based on available information`; `details are limited` | state which sources and dates were checked and what is missing | a dated research-limit report |
| Rewrite | `In conclusion`, `In summary`, `To sum up`, `Overall`, `Final thoughts` followed by repetition | close with the decision, result, unresolved question, or next action | a long teaching or policy summary |
| Remove | `As we move/look forward`; `the path ahead`; `by embracing`; `one thing is clear` | give the owner, date, scheduled step, or remaining risk | a speech followed by a concrete commitment |
| Remove | `the future is bright`; `possibilities are endless`; `never been a better time`; `only time will tell` | give a bounded forecast, next test, or stopping point | quotation, parody, intentional sentiment |
| Rewrite | `Despite its strengths, X faces challenges`; `Challenges and Future Directions`; `Future Outlook` | name present constraints and assigned future work | a report with documented risks and owners |
| Rewrite | `The key takeaway/bottom line is`; `what this means is` | state the decision once and attach it to the evidence | executive summary or spoken briefing |
| Remove | `at the end of the day`; `all in all`; `it bears mentioning`; generic `the reality is that` | end or continue with the actual claim | dialogue or a deliberate voice sample |
| Rewrite | `Save this for later`; `Bookmark this`; `Don't sleep on this`; `Trust me`; `Thank me later` | state why the resource is useful to a named reader, or omit the engagement prompt | a channel where a direct save instruction has a real function |

Email before:

> I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out regarding the revised budget and kindly ask for your thoughts at your earliest convenience.

Email after:

> Could you approve the revised budget by Thursday? The contractor cannot start until we confirm the amount.

The direct version is still courteous because it respects the reader's time and explains the dependency.

## Academic and institutional boilerplate

Academic prose needs exact scope and disciplinary terms, not a different coat of ceremony. Apply these rules to the framing around the research, never to a required method, defined construct, citation, or cautious result.

| Action | Family | Natural repair | Protect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rewrite | `this study aims/seeks to explore/investigate`; `the present study examines` | state the research question, comparison, population, method, or corpus | a required abstract structure or protocol field |
| Rewrite | `gain/provide a deeper, better, or comprehensive understanding`; `contributes to our understanding` | state the finding, distinction, or decision the work supports | a bounded synthesis that names what is now understood |
| Rewrite | `opens new avenues`; `paves the way for future research`; `future research is needed` | name the unresolved variable, population, method, or next test | a limitations section with a specific proposed study |
| Review | `a growing body of literature`; `increasing scholarly attention`; `has garnered significant interest` | cite the review, date range, publication count, or named debate | a sourced bibliometric trend |
| Rewrite | `raises important questions`; `it is clear/evident`; `there is no doubt`; `needless to say` | state the question, evidence, degree of certainty, or consequence | a formal proof or clearly attributed position |
| Review | self-awarding evaluation words such as `commendable`, `noteworthy`, `invaluable`, `versatile`, `ingenious`, `laudable`, `successfully`, `effectively`, or `convincingly` | report the criterion, comparison, observed outcome, or reviewer's attributed judgement | a measured result or direct assessment by a named evaluator |

| Action | Family | Natural repair | Protect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rewrite | `nestled in the heart of`; `bustling`; `breathtaking`; `vibrant hub`; `thriving community` | locate the place and give the street, terrain, institution, event, population, or activity that creates the impression | an attributed description or the writer's observed scene |
| Rewrite | `boasts a rich history`; `steeped in history`; `from humble beginnings` | give the date, origin, conflict, founder, trade, or documented development | a quotation or a memoir's deliberate phrasing |

| Rewrite | `a beacon of hope/progress`; `ushers in a new era`; `opens a new chapter`; `far-reaching implications` | name the later action, adoption, consequence, affected group, and time horizon | a sourced historical judgement or deliberate speech rhetoric |

## Genre calibration

- **Technical and scientific prose:** preserve distribution shift, phase shift, band gap, statistical significance, robust statistics, critical care, models, frameworks, and other terms of art. Ask whether the term is defined and used accurately.
- **News and analysis:** `policy shift` may be concise when the story immediately says what changed. Do not add historical importance that reporting cannot support.
- **Academic prose:** a research gap is useful when the missing population, method, comparison, or evidence is explicit. `This paper fills an important gap` is not enough.
- **Marketing and corporate prose:** apply remove and rewrite rules aggressively. Benefit, proof, scale, leadership, and assurance claims need mechanisms and evidence.
- **Email and support:** a warm greeting or offer of further help can be natural when a relationship or support channel is real. Remove inherited wrappers that bury the ask.
- **Essays, speeches, and narrative:** preserve deliberate metaphor, cadence, ambiguity, and the writer's own repeated motif. Remove prefab morals and stage directions, not personality.
- **UI and documentation:** user-facing prose labels or messages may be revised only when the user asks for that wording. Preserve keys, identifiers, selectors, commands, delimiters, examples, syntax, and every other machine-readable construct byte-for-byte.
- **Legal, policy, and regulated text:** exact required language, defined terms, and house style win. Record deliberate exceptions.
- **Accessible and second-language writing:** do not erase useful repetition, plain transitions, dialect, or stable phrasing merely to create statistical irregularity.

## Final avoidance check

Before delivering prose, ask:

1. Did any remove-by-default wrapper survive without a genre reason?
2. Does any rewrite-by-default phrase still carry the claim?
3. Did vague absence, change, or topic containers—such as `gap`, `shift`, `landscape`, or `journey`—become a named fact, comparison, sequence, or before-and-after?
4. Did borrowed importance become evidence, consequence, or a clearly attributed judgement?
5. Did benefit and assurance language become user actions, mechanisms, test conditions, or bounded results?
6. Did generic authority and actorless action gain a source, responsible party, scope, or honest uncertainty?
7. Do the opening, transitions, rhetorical scaffolds, and ending serve this genre rather than expose a template?
8. Did a rejected phrase merely receive a fashionable synonym?
9. Were exact technical, legal, literal, quoted, and writer-owned uses preserved without invented specificity?
10. Does the result read naturally for its actual genre rather than as a checklist of avoided words?

The pass succeeds when the thought is clearer and more owned—not when the draft merely contains fewer fashionable tokens.
